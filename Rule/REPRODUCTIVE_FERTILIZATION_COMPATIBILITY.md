# Reproductive Fertilization Compatibility

## Purpose

This file is the repository-wide authority for deciding whether the **fertilization methods of two otherwise eligible sexual species can biologically interact**.

It works with:

- `REPRODUCTION_AND_LINEAGE.md`
- `REPRODUCTIVE_SPECIES_PROFILES.md`
- `REPRODUCTIVE_SPECIES_COMPLETE_PROFILES.md`
- `REPRODUCTIVE_SPECIES_CANDIDATE_CATALOG.md`

This is homebrew biology. It does not claim that ordinary real-world species barriers apply unchanged to fantasy species.

## Core compatibility test

A proposed biological pairing must still have:

1. one biologically male contributor capable of providing the required fertilizing contribution
2. one biologically female contributor capable of providing the required ovum-equivalent contribution
3. a workable fertilization pathway
4. a viable development pathway after fertilization
5. any required carrier, host, environment, incubation, or hatching conditions

Passing fertilization compatibility does not guarantee conception. Conception probability is a later mechanic.

## Internal fertilization cross-family rule

Two completed species that both use **internal biological fertilization** are normally `Cross-species compatible` under this homebrew system when:

- the male contribution can fertilize the female contribution
- both participants have functioning anatomy for the applicable internal transfer/fertilization pathway
- neither species profile establishes a specific incompatibility
- the resulting offspring can follow the female carrier's viable biological development method

The species do **not** have to use the same development method after fertilization.

Therefore an internal-fertilizing live-bearing species can crossbreed with an internal-fertilizing egg-laying species when the other requirements are satisfied.

### Female development rule

The biologically female contributor normally determines the early developmental route:

- **live-bearing female** -> internal gestation followed by live birth
- **egg-laying female** -> egg formation, laying, incubation, then hatching
- **host-dependent female pathway** -> follows that established host/implantation biology

The offspring's eventual phenotype is resolved separately by hybrid inheritance.

## Internal live-bearing × internal egg-laying

This pairing is explicitly valid when both completed profiles allow cross-species fertilization.

Examples:

- male Human + female Dragon -> fertilized hybrid egg; female Dragon lays the egg
- male Dragon + female Elf -> live pregnancy; female Elf gives live birth
- male Orc + female Aarakocra -> fertilized hybrid egg if the Aarakocra profile is otherwise compatible
- male Lizardfolk + female Human -> live pregnancy if the Human profile is otherwise compatible

A live-born hybrid does not need to express rigid horns, heavy scales, wings, plates, spines, large claws, or other difficult structures fully at birth. `REPRODUCTION_AND_LINEAGE.md` governs delayed developmental expression.

## Internal egg-laying × internal egg-laying

Two internally fertilizing egg-laying species may be `Naturally compatible` when they are the same or very closely related species, or `Cross-species compatible` when their completed profiles permit it.

The female's egg biology determines:

- shell or membrane type
- laying requirements
- egg size within her viable reproductive range
- incubation environment
- normal pre-hatching developmental conditions

The other parent's ancestry does not force the egg to use the other species' shell or incubation method.

## Internal live-bearing × internal live-bearing

Two internally fertilizing live-bearing species may be `Naturally compatible` when they are the same or sufficiently close lineage, or `Cross-species compatible` when their completed profiles permit it.

The female carrier determines the pregnancy and delivery pathway.

Prenatal size stays within the carrier's viable range. Larger adult size inherited from the other parent can develop after birth.

## External fertilization

External-fertilization species are **not automatically compatible** with internal-fertilization species or with every other external-fertilization species.

An external pairing is `Conditionally compatible` only when all relevant conditions work, including:

- both reproductive contributions can be released or transferred into the required environment
- the fertilizing contribution remains viable in that environment
- the ovum/egg remains viable in that environment
- the contributions can physically meet and fertilize
- salinity, temperature, water, air, substrate, pressure, or other environmental requirements are satisfied
- post-fertilization development has a viable path

A Human male, for example, is not automatically compatible with an externally fertilizing aquatic species merely because both are sexual species. The aquatic profile must establish that the Human contribution can actually participate in its external fertilization pathway.

## External × internal conditional bridge

An external-fertilization species and an internal-fertilization species can qualify only when a specific biological bridge exists.

Examples of possible bridges include:

- the internal-fertilizing partner can provide viable reproductive material externally
- the externally fertilizing partner's eggs can be fertilized with that contribution in the required environment
- the resulting fertilized egg can then develop externally or be transferred into a biologically suitable carrier

Do not invent this bridge merely to force compatibility. It must follow the completed profiles or an explicit homebrew species fact.

## Host-dependent / implanted development

Host-dependent development is separate from genetic parentage.

### Fertilization before implantation

If an egg or embryo is fertilized before implantation:

- the host does not need to be a genetic parent
- the host needs only the anatomy and physiology required to support development
- the host cannot alter genetic parentage merely by carrying the offspring

### Fertilization after implantation

If fertilization must occur after implantation:

- the host must biologically support the required fertilization pathway
- implantation does not grant reproductive anatomy the host does not possess

A Human male carrying an unfertilized implanted egg therefore cannot fertilize it merely because he is the host unless the species' established biology provides a real fertilization mechanism.

## Carrier-safe development

Fertilization compatibility does not override physical development limits.

For a viable pairing:

- prenatal/pre-laying size follows the carrier's safe biological range
- rigid or dangerous inherited structures may remain immature until after birth or hatching
- the carrying parent's development method is preserved unless a specific profile says otherwise

If even delayed development cannot produce a viable carrier-safe pathway, the pairing becomes `Conditionally compatible` or `Incompatible by ordinary biology` as appropriate.

## Naturally compatible vs Cross-species compatible

Use `Naturally compatible` primarily for:

- same species
- subspecies/lineages that share essentially the same reproductive biology
- source-established close pairings that the repository explicitly classifies that way

Use `Cross-species compatible` for biologically different species that pass the fertilization and development tests.

Use `Conditionally compatible` when a required environment, host, anatomy, incubation condition, or other non-magical biological condition must be satisfied.

Do not use Creature Type, edition, power level, divine status, or publication source as the deciding factor.

## No synthetic randomness

This compatibility engine determines biological possibility. It does not roll for success.

When later fertility or conception mechanics require randomness, all dice are physically rolled by the player under `DICE_ROLLS_AND_REROLLS.md`. ChatGPT must never invent a conception roll or choose a random result to fit the story.
