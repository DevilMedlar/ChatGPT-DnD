# Reproductive Species Candidate Catalog

## Purpose

This file is the repository-wide **open candidate registry** for species that may fit the biological breeding rules in `REPRODUCTION_AND_LINEAGE.md` and `REPRODUCTIVE_SPECIES_PROFILES.md`.

The acceptance test is:

> **Sexual reproduction + biologically male/female reproductive roles + workable fertilization + viable development + a Naturally compatible, Cross-species compatible, or actually satisfied Conditionally compatible pairing = biologically eligible.**

Being listed here does **not** mean a species can breed with every other listed species. It means the species is worth profiling because it plausibly can satisfy the global biological gate.

This is a homebrew-universal catalog. Current, Legacy, older-edition, future, setting-specific, monster/NPC, third-party, homebrew, futuristic, extraterrestrial, divine, demi-divine, draconic, giant, fey, celestial, fiendish, aberrant, or otherwise unusual species are all allowed to enter the registry when their biology plausibly fits.

No finite document can literally contain species that have not been invented or published yet. Therefore this catalog is **open-ended by rule**: any newly encountered species is added when it plausibly passes the same biological test.

## Deduplication rule

Keep **one canonical row per distinct species or materially different biological version**.

- Exact repeats are removed.
- Aliases for the same biological lineage are combined in one row.
- Source or setting variants that use the same reproductive biology are named in the canonical row's note rather than repeated elsewhere.
- A genuinely different version stays separate when its biology, lifecycle, fertility, or reproductive origin materially differs.
- Species are organized by biological/reproductive family rather than repeated again in a separate source-based section.

## Status meanings

- **Candidate**: sexual male/female reproduction is established or strongly biologically supported; full pairing compatibility still requires a completed profile.
- **Candidate — conditional**: reproduction plausibly works but depends on an environmental, anatomical, host, incubation, aquatic, or other non-magical biological condition.
- **Hold**: the species might fit, but sexual reproduction, sex roles, fertilization, or development is not established well enough yet.
- **Not a breeder under current global rules**: known biology conflicts with the current restricted breeding pool. These are tracked only where useful to prevent accidental inclusion.

## Important interpretation rules

1. **Creature Type is not a gate.** Humanoid, Fey, Dragon, Giant, Celestial, Fiend, Aberration, Monstrosity, or another type can qualify.
2. **Power is not a gate.** A mortal, dragon, giant, demigod-like being, or god-like species uses the same biological test.
3. **Publication status is not a gate.** Legacy and old-edition species are as valid for research as current species.
4. **A divine title is not automatically a species.** A god or demigod who belongs to an established biological species uses that species profile. A truly distinct divine species receives its own profile only if it has coherent biological sexual reproduction.
5. **Hybrid names can be evidence of compatibility.** Half-elf, half-orc, half-drow, half-sea-elf, half-goblin, half-ogre, Khoravar, Jhorgun'taal, trollkin, cambion, and similar lineages demonstrate that a parent pairing has existed in at least some D&D or partner lore. This repository's own inheritance rules determine the actual offspring result.
6. **Shapechanger status remains excluded under the current global rule** when the species itself is a shapechanger or reproduction depends on shapechanging.
7. **Asexual-only, magical-only, constructed-only, undead-only, or otherwise non-biological reproduction remains outside the initial breeding pool.**
8. **Animal-model inference is allowed** when a sapient fantasy species is plainly modeled on a real mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian, fish, insect, arachnid, or similar organism and no established lore contradicts that model.

## Automatic future intake rule

Whenever a new species appears in a future D&D book, older source, monster manual, partner book, homebrew setting, science-fantasy source, divine cosmology, or campaign creation, ChatGPT should ask only:

1. Is the species sapient enough for adult agency and consent?
2. Does the species normally reproduce sexually?
3. Does it have biologically male and female reproductive roles under the current global scope?
4. Is there a workable internal, external, implanted, or other non-magical fertilization path?
5. Is there a viable live-bearing, egg-laying, host-dependent, aquatic, larval, or other biological development path?
6. Can the proposed pairing satisfy `Naturally compatible`, `Cross-species compatible`, or `Conditionally compatible` requirements?

If yes, add it as a Candidate and build a reproductive profile. If uncertain, add it as Hold. If no, it remains outside the initial breeding pool unless a campaign-specific override changes the applicable rule.
