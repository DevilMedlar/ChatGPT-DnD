# Reproductive Species Complete Profiles — Batch 6

## Purpose

This batch records strong cross-species evidence from Ogre, Orc, Goblinoid, and Human hybrid lineages and establishes a reusable **directional hybrid-expression precedent**.

The important distinction is:

> Biological compatibility may be symmetric while the **named lineage, body plan, or trait expression of the offspring can be directional** depending on which species is the female carrier and which species is the male contributor.

This does not replace the later hybrid-inheritance system. It gives that system established examples to preserve.

All fertilization uses `REPRODUCTIVE_FERTILIZATION_COMPATIBILITY.md`.

---

# Ogre cross-species compatibility network

Published D&D material establishes that Ogres can produce Half-Ogre offspring with multiple other biological peoples, including Humans, Hobgoblins, Bugbears, and Orcs.

Under this repository:

- Ogre + Human is source-established compatibility
- Ogre + Orc is source-established compatibility
- Ogre + Hobgoblin is source-established compatibility
- Ogre + Bugbear is source-established compatibility
- all four pairings use internal biological fertilization and live-bearing development
- the female carrier determines pregnancy and safe neonatal size
- old source statements about Human mothers rarely surviving do not override the repository's universal carrier-safe development rule; a campaign can restore harsher legacy obstetric risk if desired

The old source statement that Ogres do not mate with Dwarves, Halflings, or Elves is treated as a source-culture/behavior statement rather than automatic proof of biological impossibility, because its own explanation is behavioral predation rather than reproductive anatomy. Those pairings therefore remain eligible for the repository's normal internal-fertilization compatibility test unless a version-specific biological profile proves actual incompatibility.

# Ogrillon

## Ogrillon — directional Ogre/Orc lineage

- **Species:** Ogrillon
- **Source / Version:** Legacy D&D Ogre/Orc hybrid lineage
- **Creature Type:** Humanoid, Giant/Orc hybrid, or edition-equivalent
- **Profile Status:** Complete as hybrid evidence
- **Evidence Basis:** Published lore identifies Ogrillons as Ogre/Orc offspring; some Legacy lore specifically associates Ogrillon with male Ogre + female Orc parentage
- **Parent Pairing:** Ogre + Orc
- **Directional Legacy Pattern:** male Ogre + female Orc -> Ogrillon
- **Reproductive Mode of Parents:** Sexual
- **Fertilization Method:** Internal
- **Development Method:** Live-bearing through the female Orc carrier in the directional Legacy pattern
- **Carrier / Host:** Female Orc
- **Birth / Hatching Constraints:** carrier-safe Orc-scale newborn; Ogre-derived mass, strength, bony plates, and larger adult frame develop progressively after birth
- **General Parent Compatibility:** Cross-species compatible
- **Later-Generation Fertility:** version-specific; some Legacy sources call Ogrillons sterile, while other third-party/homebrew versions allow stable Ogrillon populations. Do not lock the repository-wide offspring-fertility rule from this contradiction yet
- **Notes:** Ogrillon demonstrates that hybrid phenotype can be strongly directional without changing the fact that Ogre and Orc are reproductively compatible.

# Directional hybrid-expression precedent

The Ogrillon/Orog tradition establishes a general homebrew principle for later inheritance mechanics:

For the same two parent species, reversing which species is male and which is female may influence:

- initial body plan
- neonatal size
- gestational expression
- which ancestry's structures are delayed until later growth
- dominant cosmetic phenotype
- named lineage used by a culture
- trait-expression probabilities

However, parent direction does **not automatically change**:

- whether the two species are biologically compatible
- the fact that both are genetic parents
- the offspring's right to inherit traits from either ancestry

Exact directional inheritance percentages remain deferred until the hybrid-inheritance system is designed.

---

# Source-specific sterility is not yet global

Some historical hybrid lineages are described as sterile in one source and fertile or self-sustaining in another.

Until `offspring fertility` is formally designed:

- record source-specific sterility as evidence
- do not automatically make all hybrids sterile
- do not automatically make all hybrids fertile
- do not use sterility to retroactively invalidate the parents' ability to produce the first-generation hybrid

First-generation compatibility and later-generation fertility are separate mechanics.
